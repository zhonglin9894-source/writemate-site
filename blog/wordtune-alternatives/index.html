5 Wordtune Alternatives in 2025 — Better Value for ESL Writers

By NativeProse Team · July 1, 2025 · 7 min read

Wordtune fills a specific niche. You write something, feel like it doesn't sound quite right, and you want to see four or five alternative ways to phrase the same sentence. For that exact use case, it's genuinely decent. But $13.99 a month is a significant ask for a tool that works one sentence at a time — especially when you need to rewrite a whole email or document.

If you're looking to switch, the right alternative depends on why Wordtune isn't working for you. Is the price the issue? The sentence-by-sentence limitation? Or is the deeper problem that your English needs to sound native, not just different? The answers point to different tools.

1
NativeProse — If the goal is sounding native

Wordtune's output is often grammatically fine but doesn't necessarily sound like something a native speaker would write. It swaps words and restructures sentences, but the underlying patterns — the slightly formal register, the translated idioms, the over-hedged phrasing — can survive the rewrite unchanged.

NativeProse works differently. Instead of showing you five alternatives to pick from, it rewrites the text the way a native speaker would actually phrase it. The output isn't just different — it's calibrated to sound natural in the way native English readers expect. It handles full paragraphs at once (not sentence by sentence), and works directly in any browser text field — Gmail, Slack, LinkedIn, Google Docs — without copy-pasting.

At $9/month with 10 free rewrites per day, it's cheaper than Wordtune and better suited to the specific problem most ESL writers actually have.

Choose this if: You're a non-native speaker who needs your English to sound genuinely natural, not just grammatically varied.
2
LanguageTool — If price is the main concern

LanguageTool is the most capable free Grammarly alternative, and at $6.99/month for premium it's the cheapest of the serious options. It catches grammar errors well across 30+ languages, and the premium plan adds style improvements and clarity suggestions.

It won't rewrite sentences for you the way Wordtune does, and it doesn't focus on naturalness for non-native speakers. But if what you mostly need is grammar checking with occasional style nudges, the price is hard to beat.

Choose this if: You need solid grammar checking on a tight budget and don't need full sentence rewrites.
3
QuillBot — If you're paraphrasing, not nativizing

QuillBot is Wordtune's closest direct competitor in terms of what it does. It paraphrases text, restructures sentences, and offers multiple modes (Standard, Fluency, Formal, Creative). The free plan is usable though limited.

The honest caveat: QuillBot is great at making text look different. It's less great at making text sound like a native speaker wrote it — the outputs can feel generic, and the tool doesn't specifically target the patterns that make ESL writing sound foreign. If you need to paraphrase academic content or restructure source material, it's useful. If you need professional emails to sound natural, it'll do half the job.

Choose this if: You're paraphrasing existing text (academic work, content rewriting) rather than trying to improve naturalism.
4
Hemingway Editor — If clarity is the actual problem

Hemingway doesn't rewrite anything. It highlights complexity — long sentences, passive voice, adverbs, dense phrasing — and gives you a readability grade. You do the rewriting yourself. The free web version is genuinely useful as a final polish step for blog posts or articles.

If Wordtune was feeling like overkill and what you really need is a "make this clearer" signal, Hemingway is free and surprisingly effective. It won't help with the foreign-sounding patterns specific to ESL writers, but for general clarity it earns its keep.

Choose this if: You want to simplify your writing and improve readability, and you're comfortable doing the actual rewrites yourself.
5
ChatGPT — If you want total flexibility

ChatGPT can rewrite anything in any style if you give it clear instructions. "Rewrite this email to sound like a native English speaker in a professional but warm tone" works well. The free plan (GPT-3.5) is capable enough for most rewriting tasks; GPT-4o produces noticeably better output.

The friction is the workflow. You need to switch tabs, write a prompt, paste your text, copy the output back. For occasional use that's fine. For rewriting text regularly across multiple apps — Gmail, Slack, Notion, LinkedIn — it gets tedious fast. There's also no browser extension, so it doesn't work inside text fields directly.

Choose this if: You need flexible, high-quality rewrites for occasional use and don't mind the copy-paste workflow.

The Bottom Line

Wordtune is a decent tool with an awkward pricing model. Most people looking for an alternative fall into one of two camps: they want something cheaper (LanguageTool, QuillBot), or they want something that solves a deeper problem — making their English sound native, not just grammatically varied (NativeProse).

If you've been using Wordtune and still feeling like your writing sounds a bit foreign even after rewrites, that's a sign the tool isn't solving the right problem. The sentence-level alternatives Wordtune offers are varied, but they're working within the same register and phrasing style as your original — they're not replacing it with how a native speaker would actually say it.
