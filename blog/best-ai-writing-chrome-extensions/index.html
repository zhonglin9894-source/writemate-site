2. Grammarly — Best for Grammar Correction

2
Grammarly
Grammar King

Grammarly is the most installed writing extension on Chrome, and for good reason. It catches grammar mistakes, spelling errors, and punctuation issues in real time across almost every website. The inline suggestions are clear and easy to accept with one click.

The free plan handles basic grammar and spelling. The premium plan ($12/month) adds tone detection, clarity improvements, and some style suggestions. The Business plan adds team features.

The main limitation: Grammarly makes your writing correct, but it doesn't make it sound natural. Non-native speakers often find their Grammarly-edited text still feels stiff or formal in unintended ways. Grammarly fixes rule violations — it doesn't replace word choices with what a native speaker would actually say.

✓ Pros
  • Best-in-class grammar detection
  • Works on almost every website
  • Inline suggestions (easy one-click accept)
  • Strong free plan for basic checking
✗ Cons
  • Doesn't fix "sounds foreign" problem
  • Premium is expensive ($12/mo)
  • Can be intrusive / over-corrective
  • AI features require premium
Best for: Native English speakers who need a safety net for grammar and typos. Less useful for non-native speakers who want to sound more fluent.

3. Wordtune — Best for Sentence-Level Rewrites

3
Wordtune
Sentence Alternatives

Wordtune's core feature is offering multiple alternative phrasings for any sentence you select. Highlight a sentence, click Wordtune, and you get 5–8 different ways to say the same thing — ranging from casual to formal.

This is useful when you know what you want to say but can't find the right words. The downside is that it works sentence by sentence, which makes rewriting a full email or document slow and tedious. At $13.99/month, it's also one of the pricier options for what it does.

✓ Pros
  • Multiple alternatives to compare
  • Good for finding the right phrasing
  • Casual/formal tone toggle
✗ Cons
  • Sentence-by-sentence only (slow for full docs)
  • Pricier than alternatives
  • Free plan limited to 10 rewrites/day
Best for: Writers who want to compare alternative phrasings while drafting, rather than full rewrites.

4. Compose AI — Best for Email Autocomplete

4
Compose AI
Autocomplete Focus

Compose AI takes a different approach: instead of rewriting what you've written, it autocompletes as you type — similar to Gmail's Smart Compose, but more powerful. It learns your writing style and suggests full sentence completions inline.

It's genuinely useful for writing repetitive emails quickly. But it doesn't improve the quality of your writing — if your natural English has patterns that sound non-native, autocomplete will just repeat those patterns faster.

✓ Pros
  • Speeds up email writing significantly
  • Works well in Gmail
  • Free plan available
✗ Cons
  • Doesn't improve writing quality
  • Can feel intrusive while typing
  • Limited to certain sites
Best for: High-volume email writers who want to type faster, not write better.

5. Jasper — Best for Marketing & Long-Form Content

5
Jasper
Marketing Teams

Jasper is an enterprise-focused AI writing platform with a Chrome extension for browser-based writing. It's built for marketing teams generating large volumes of content — blog posts, ad copy, product descriptions, social media.

At $39/month minimum, it's completely overkill for individuals. The extension itself is relatively limited compared to the full Jasper web app. For anyone writing personal or professional English, this is not the right tool.

✓ Pros
  • Powerful for marketing content
  • 50+ templates for different content types
  • Brand voice training
✗ Cons
  • Extremely expensive ($39/mo minimum)
  • No free plan
  • Overkill for individual users
Best for: Marketing teams with content budgets. Not suitable for individual ESL writers or students.
